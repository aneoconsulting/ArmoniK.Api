s17, a follow-up probe of the grid's largest difference (CONTAINER INSTRUMENTATION, NOT quiet:
load1 about 1.0 when it ran; one process per build and tiering setting, BDN's grouped
toolchain, AK_BDN_ONLY=P1.1,U-nested-before, encode-core-hot, E0, drop and retain, 6 rounds).
The grid (one child process per case) shows retain ~300-600 ns faster with the lazy render on
the small rows that have elements (P1.1, P2.1, the U-* rows), drop about equal.
Here, ns per op (median of rounds):
  eager  TC=1: P1.1 drop 805 retain 1135; U-nested-before drop 233 retain 565
  lazy   TC=1: P1.1 drop 709 retain 1014; U-nested-before drop 214 retain 393
  eager  TC=0: P1.1 drop 782 retain 1108; U-nested-before drop 248 retain 563
  lazy   TC=0: P1.1 drop 777 retain  818; U-nested-before drop 257 retain 250
The ~300 ns retain excess is present for the eager render with and without tiered compilation,
and present or absent for the lazy render depending on the process (here: with TC=1 in a
grouped process it appears for lazy too). So it is a process-state-dependent cost of the
retain encode, not attributed to the render; the grid's retain differences between the two
renders are not explained by these runs. Counts and bytes of the two renders are identical
(logs/csharp/opt/s17/p2).
